**The deck’s engine worked well across the 7–2 run. The main improvement is recognizing when another turn of setup helps the opponent more than it helps you.** That means developing sooner in some positions, attacking sooner in others, and preserving protection when spending it does not secure your winning route.

The Proft correction stands: **I no longer classify letting Unsummon resolve in Game 4 as a mistake.** The opponent challenging known Teyo changes the evaluation substantially.

**Game 1 — improve color access, then recognize the finishing window.**

At [your turn 2](https://www.17lands.com/history/e187816eba1f41c78c0d0c57aa13ce26/0/0/8), you have Plains and Forest available, several blue spells stranded, and Hexhaven Battalion in hand. There is no competing productive two-mana play.

Cycle Battalion for Island before your next untap. Keeping this hand is reasonable partly because Battalion provides that escape from missing blue; delaying the cycling gives up that benefit. The downside is losing Battalion’s eventual token production, but functional early turns matter more here. **Clear error, high confidence.**

At [your turn 13, after Plan removes Woodwork Prodigy](https://www.17lands.com/history/e187816eba1f41c78c0d0c57aa13ce26/0/0/251), the opponent is at 10. You have five ready 2/2 Cadets, a 5/5 Mind Meanderer, and enough mana for Aspirant. Their only available blocker is 8/8 Ruric Thar.

Cast Aspirant, put its counter and temporary indestructibility on Meanderer, then attack with the resulting 6/6 and all five Cadets:

- Ruric blocks Meanderer: the Cadets deal **10**.
- Ruric blocks a Cadet: Meanderer and the other Cadets deal **14**.

That is lethal through the visible board. It is **not guaranteed lethal through three unknown cards and open mana**; removal before the Aspirant trigger resolves is a meaningful risk. Nevertheless, you are at 18, retain Aspirant, and Meanderer remains available to block if it survives. Waiting gives the opponent additional draws and development.

**Preferred line, high confidence.** This is where attacking sooner reduces overall risk while ahead.

**Game 2 — the most consequential improvement is stabilizing the air before building more value.**

At [opponent turn 8, after Recursive Recruitment](https://www.17lands.com/history/e187816eba1f41c78c0d0c57aa13ce26/1/0/131), the opponent is fully tapped out. You have seven unused mana and Divining Duelist in hand.

Flash Duelist before combat and tap Blossom-Blessed Angel. This develops a body and prevents its attack; alternatively, an end-step Duelist still converts otherwise unused mana into development. Saving Duelist preserves flexibility, but you are allowing their board to grow while yours stalls.

**Clear missed development window, high confidence.** Holding a flash creature should have a specific payoff; it need not wait indefinitely for maximum value.

At [your turn 9](https://www.17lands.com/history/e187816eba1f41c78c0d0c57aa13ce26/1/0/140), the opponent remains tapped out and you draw Mind Meanderer. Their Angel is 2/4, and Surveillance Phantasm presents a second aerial attacker.

You instead cast Way of the Mind Sculptor, activate Jace, and cast the drawn Proft. That produces useful resources, but leaves no flying blocker. The opponent can activate Phantasm and send both flyers at your four-loyalty Jace.

I prefer:

1. Cast Meanderer for six mana.
2. Fight and kill Angel immediately.
3. Keep Meanderer as an aerial blocker through their next turn.
4. Develop Way afterward, with a better chance of sustaining its loyalty engine.

The opponent can draw or use removal after untapping, so Meanderer does not guarantee stabilization. However, Angel is already gone, and answering Meanderer consumes another resource. The cost is postponing Way’s immediate card and token production.

**Preferred line, high confidence.** Your limiting resource here is defense against flyers, not access to more cards.

**Game 3 — being at one life did not mean you should spend every available resource.**

At [your turn 11](https://www.17lands.com/history/e187816eba1f41c78c0d0c57aa13ce26/2/0/208), Gideon the Oathless makes your creature entries and loyalty activations deal damage to you. You are at one life, with Thalia and Aspirant in hand.

Holding those creatures is correct. Thalia’s lifelink cannot rescue you from the entry trigger, and Aspirant’s value does not prevent that lethal damage.

Your existing board supplies time to find an answer to Gideon and reopen your engine. An opposing burn spell could still end the game, but casting a creature yourself loses immediately.

**Good play, high confidence.** Assess the route to winning from the whole position; a low life total alone does not justify taking additional risks.

**Game 4 — distinguish a questionable keep from a defensible protection decision.**

For the [opening hand](https://www.17lands.com/history/e187816eba1f41c78c0d0c57aa13ce26/3/0/0), I prefer a mulligan. Plains–Forest leaves Phantasm, Proft, and Foreseer uncastable, while Keeper and Rescue Girl also require another land. There is no Battalion to fix the missing color.

The hand becomes strong with the right draw, but too much of its functionality depends on that draw. Going to six costs a card, so this is a **preferred mulligan, medium confidence**, rather than an indisputable error.

At [your turn 9 end step, Unsummon targeting Proft](https://www.17lands.com/history/e187816eba1f41c78c0d0c57aa13ce26/3/0/161), the opponent knows about Teyo. Their action therefore deserves more scrutiny than an ordinary bounce spell.

Two relevant explanations are:

- **Immediate follow-up:** counter Teyo. You have four mana; casting Teyo leaves two, insufficient to pay Icy Reception’s three-mana tax.
- **Delayed follow-up:** induce Teyo now, then answer Proft after its temporary hexproof expires. Any proposed removal must actually handle the enlarged Proft and be castable with their colors.

Allowing Unsummon to resolve preserves both cards, but costs four counters and replay tempo. Casting Teyo could also be worthwhile if exchanging it for a counter clears the way for your next important threat.

**Close choice; your line is defensible.** The bait hypothesis supports caution, but does not automatically prove declining is best. The earlier “protect Proft” criticism was too strong.

**Game 5 — the opening is the clearest actionable issue.**

The [opening seven](https://www.17lands.com/history/e187816eba1f41c78c0d0c57aa13ce26/4/0/0) contains two Plains, Forest, Phantasm, Duelist, Way of the Healer, and Way of the Mind Sculptor.

You have land quantity, but no blue access and no immediate play. Even Healer needs a fourth land. Being on the draw makes this closer than Game 4, yet I still prefer looking for a hand that can affect the board earlier.

**Preferred mulligan, medium confidence.** The eventual loss does not establish that the keep was wrong; the concern is its initial dependence on specific draws.

Once behind against the opponent’s pressure and recurring damage, simply generating more cards was not enough to establish a durable recovery. I do not see a sufficiently robust later alternative to claim that one tactical change would have reversed this loss.

**Game 6 — a good example of converting development directly into a winning clock.**

At [your turn 9](https://www.17lands.com/history/e187816eba1f41c78c0d0c57aa13ce26/5/0/146), the opponent is at nine, Variable Chaser is their available blocker, and you have three Cadets.

Meanderer fights Chaser, allowing the Cadets to hit for six and leaving a flying threat. Battalion would add more material, but would not remove the immediate obstruction as efficiently.

The opponent has only one untapped land, making this a favorable window. Even a later answer to Meanderer does not undo the removed blocker or six damage.

**Good play, high confidence.** You correctly prioritized converting your existing board over accumulating more of it.

**Game 7 — good stabilization, followed by an opportunity to start the removal loop earlier.**

At [your turn 7](https://www.17lands.com/history/e187816eba1f41c78c0d0c57aa13ce26/6/0/97), making Aspirant a 4/5 is valuable against the opposing 4/4 Beast. Returning Tomik would leave only a 3/4 Aspirant, and your six available mana cannot cast both Aspirant and the returned Tomik that turn.

**Good play, high confidence.** The counter mode supplies the blocker you need immediately.

Later, at [your turn 12](https://www.17lands.com/history/e187816eba1f41c78c0d0c57aa13ce26/6/0/197), you have twelve available mana sources, Plan, Teyo, and Duelist in hand, plus Rescue Girl already on the battlefield.

I prefer casting Plan on the Beast now. It permanently removes a token, leaves eight mana—including protection—and positions Plan for a Rescue Girl reuse next turn. Passing preserves Plan’s flexibility against a new threat, but delays progress despite ample room to act and protect your engine.

**Preferred line, medium confidence.** The main downside is spending your flexible answer if Rescue Girl is subsequently removed; that matters, but Teyo helps cover that risk.

**Game 8 — this game supports both Foreseer and Aspirant’s roles.**

At [your turn 12](https://www.17lands.com/history/e187816eba1f41c78c0d0c57aa13ce26/7/0/210), you are at seven against a 6/4 and a 3/3, with two of your creatures disabled by Coursework.

Foreseer, its prepared Peer Review, and your loyalty activation add the bodies needed to defend while retaining the card engine. The opponent has no cards in hand at that point, so committing resources to this stabilization is well supported.

At [your turn 15, Aspirant’s entry trigger](https://www.17lands.com/history/e187816eba1f41c78c0d0c57aa13ce26/7/0/309), returning Rescue Girl restores access to repeated Plan and enchantment entries. Here, recursion contributes more to the winning route than another counter.

**Both are good plays.** Together with Game 7, they demonstrate why Aspirant’s flexibility matters: sometimes it is a necessary blocker; sometimes it rebuilds your engine.

**Game 9 — strong removal and attack-target selection.**

At [your turn 8](https://www.17lands.com/history/e187816eba1f41c78c0d0c57aa13ce26/8/0/117), the opponent is tapped out. You use Plan on their untapped 2/2 Cadet, then send Tomik and your Cadet at Chandra.

That removes the token permanently and deals four combat damage to the three-loyalty planeswalker. Using Plan directly on Chandra could let the opponent redraw her; attacking their life total would leave her engine functioning.

**Good play, high confidence.** You use removal to enable combat to answer the more important permanent.

The run supports a deck with substantial late-game strength, especially through Rescue Girl and the Ways. Its practical weaknesses are early color access and the time needed to establish that engine. Aspirant earned its place through both modes, and Foreseer supplied relevant stabilization. These games do not establish that replacing Awakening with Sphinx’s Approach would improve the deck.

Three habits to practice:

1. **Before passing, name what your reserved mana is protecting—and when you will spend it if that threat never appears.**
2. **When ahead, identify the opponent’s comeback route.** Sometimes preserving protection closes it; sometimes attacking now does.
3. **When behind, describe a sequence that can actually win.** Prioritize the blocker, removal spell, or attack window that makes that sequence possible before adding more value.
